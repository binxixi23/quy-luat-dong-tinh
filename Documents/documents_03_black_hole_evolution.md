# Chapter 3: The Evolution of Black Holes: From Dynamics to Stasis

## 1. The Evolutionary Lifecycle
Black holes strictly obey the natural laws of evolution: **Feeding, Growth, Maturation (Degeneration), and Death (State Shift)**.
- **The Micro Scale:** Microscopic black holes can barely sustain themselves. The leaking of quantum energy via Hawking Radiation happens so violently that they evaporate and explode in the blink of an eye.
- **The Active Phase (Peak Dynamics):** If there is food, there is growth. A maturing black hole devours everything along its gravitational path, expanding its mass and widening its Event Horizon.
- **The Static Phase (The Turning Point):** Once a black hole overeats or the universe expands too wide—starving it of surrounding matter—it falls into a phase of decay. It consumes its own accumulated mass via radiation, grows old, and ultimately releases its remaining energy in a magnificent flash, dissolving into the silent cosmic background (**Stasis**).

## 2. "An Egg Cannot Lay Itself"
A black hole might be terrifying, but it remains an obedient child to its "foster mother"—Space-Time:
- A black hole cannot swallow the very space that contains it. It merely warps it (like a heavy iron sphere denting a rubber sheet).
- It cannot gravitationally swallow itself to erase its mass into absolute nothingness. It is bound by the strict laws of conservation of energy and the cosmic speed limit of light.
